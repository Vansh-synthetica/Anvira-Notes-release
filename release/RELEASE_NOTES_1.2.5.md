### New
- **Mock exams.** A new Mock exam card at the top of Study. Pick a subject (a notebook), a length (Quick 10, Standard 20 or Full 30 questions), timed or not, and whether to include written answers. Questions come from all over the subject's notes and files, and every new exam asks different questions from the ones before.
- **Sit it like a real exam.** No answers are shown until you hand it in. Move between questions with the question grid, flag questions to come back to, and change answers until you hand in. Answers are saved as you go: leave and continue later, and the clock stops while you are away. Before you hand in, it lists anything unanswered or flagged.
- **Results that teach.** Your score and letter grade, how each topic went, and every mistake explained from your own notes: why your answer was wrong and why the right one is right. Written answers are marked by the AI against a model answer, and you can change a mark you disagree with. One click turns your mistakes into flashcards.
- **Grades per subject.** All your mock exams in a subject add up to one grade, with newer and longer exams counting more. A range ("likely 74–88%") shows how sure it is, and narrows as you sit more exams. See every subject's grade, trend and weakest topics in **Grades**.
- **Keep studying while it's written.** On a laptop a small AI model takes a few minutes to write an exam. Choose "Keep studying meanwhile" and the Mock exam card tells you when it is ready.

### Better
- **Fair questions.** The AI answers each of its own multiple-choice questions before it goes in, and a question whose answer it cannot confirm is replaced. Questions that give their own answer away, or are not really questions, are left out. If a question still looks wrong, use **Bad question? Leave it out** in the results and it stops counting towards your score and grade.
- **Quizzes with very small models (about 0.5B).** Tiny models used to copy the instructions ("option A", "the question") instead of writing questions. They now get a worked example and write real questions. Quizzes made with bigger models are unchanged. With a very small model, mock exams are multiple choice only, and Study says plainly that a model of 1.5B or more writes better exams.
