### New: Chemistry Lab
Open it from the new card at the top of **Study**. Type any molecule (a name like *caffeine*, a formula like *SF4* or *SO4^2-*, a condensed formula like *CH3CH2OH*, or SMILES), draw one, or open a .mol, .sdf, .xyz, .pdb or .cif file.

- **3D models** you can rotate, with lone pairs, bond angles, bond lengths, dipole arrows, partial charges and electrostatic-potential surfaces. Click atoms to measure distances, angles and dihedrals.
- **Lewis structures, step by step**: counting valence electrons, placing lone pairs, forming double bonds and checking formal charges. Includes every resonance form, the resonance hybrid, and a switch between the "octet rule" and "lowest formal charges" conventions.
- **VSEPR**: AXE notation, electron and molecular geometry, hybridisation, and real bond angles (lone pairs squeeze them: water comes out near its measured 104.5°).
- **Symmetry**: the point group, with its axes and mirror planes drawn in 3D, and whether the molecule is polar or chiral (R/S and E/Z labels).
- **Spectra**: an IR spectrum with every vibration animated, estimated ¹H and ¹³C NMR, and mass-spectrum isotope patterns.
- **Properties**: molar mass, composition, functional groups, intermolecular forces, logP, polar surface area, Lipinski's rule of five, conformers, and π orbitals.
- **Explorers**: build any VSEPR shape, view atomic orbitals (1s to 6f) and electron configurations, MO diagrams for diatomics (why O₂ is paramagnetic), 12 crystal unit cells, and an interactive periodic table with trends.

Around 200 molecules and ions work offline. Any other compound is looked up on PubChem, and proteins by their PDB code (e.g. 1CRN), when you are online. Copy any picture straight into a note.

Built on OpenChemLib and 3Dmol.js (both open source, BSD licence).
